How to Use Primer PICKR

A guide to finding the perfect primers for your experiment.

Getting Started: Finding Your Primers

Primer PICKR is designed to be simple. The main page features a search bar where you can enter the official gene symbol for your target (e.g., TP53, GAPDH, Actb). Once you've entered a gene, the search button will become active.

Selecting a Species

Directly above the filters, you'll find a dropdown menu to select the species for your search. Currently, we support Human and Mouse. Make sure you select the correct species before searching to get relevant results.

Using Filters

The filter panel on the left allows you to narrow your search based on specific experimental needs. You can set ranges for melting temperature (Tm), GC content, primer length, and the final amplicon length. You can also filter by the number of literature citations or the maximum allowed difference between the forward and reverse primer melting temperatures (ΔTm).

Interpreting the Results: The Primer Card

After a successful search, results are displayed as a list of primer cards, ranked by our comprehensive PICKR Score. Here's a breakdown of what you see on each card:

  • Rank: The overall rank of the primer pair for that gene based on its PICKR Score.
  • Sequences: The forward (5' → 3') and reverse (5' → 3') primer sequences.
  • Combined Citations: The total number of papers in our database that cite either the forward or reverse primer.
  • Amplicon Length: The expected length of the PCR product in base pairs.
  • Avg Tm: The average melting temperature of the two primers.
  • PICKR Score: The overall score from 0-100, combining evidence from literature, biophysical properties, and the synergy between them. A higher score is better.

If a pair has been cited together in the same paper(s), a green "Cited Together" badge will appear, which is a strong indicator of a functional pair.

A Guide to Picking the Best Primers

While the PICKR Score is a powerful guide, the "best" primer pair often depends on your specific experimental needs. Here’s a checklist of what to look for when you expand a primer card for more details:

1. Prioritize Strong Evidence

Look for pairs with a high PICKR Score and, most importantly, a Shared Citations count greater than zero. This is the strongest evidence that the pair works well together in a lab setting.

2. Check for Off-Target Matches

The details for each primer will show the number of potential off-target matches in the genome.

  • 0 Matches: This is ideal. The primer is highly specific.
  • >0 Matches: This primer may bind to other genes. Review the details carefully.
  • Identical Matches: If you see a bold red warning for an "Identical sequence match", the primer is a perfect match for another gene and should generally be avoided to prevent non-specific amplification.

3. Evaluate Biophysical Properties

In the "Pair Details" section, look for:

  • Low ΔTm: The difference in melting temperature between the two primers should be as small as possible, ideally less than 2°C, for efficient amplification.
  • Low Complementarity: The "Self-Complementarity" and "Pair Complementarity" scores should be low to avoid the formation of primer-dimers and hairpins.

4. Consider Your Application

Finally, ensure the primer characteristics match your experiment:

  • For qPCR: Shorter amplicon lengths (e.g., 70-200 bp) are generally preferred.
  • For Cloning/Sanger Sequencing: Longer amplicon lengths may be necessary to capture the region of interest.
